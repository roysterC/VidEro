Step 00Read first

Ground rules before you start

An AI influencer is a fictional person whose photos and videos are generated, not shot. These rules come first because breaking any of them can end the account overnight, and some can get you into legal trouble.

100% fictional

No real person's face, name or photos as a base. Never prompt "looks like <celebrity>". Never use reference photos of people who haven't signed a release.

21+

Clearly adult

State an age of 21 or older and keep adult styling and contexts. Never use youthful cues such as "teen", "girl", "petite" or school uniforms, not even as a joke.

Label it as AI

Turn on each platform's AI-content toggle and say "AI creator" in the bio. Most platforms now require this, and undisclosed AI personas get removed.

Keep a generation log

Record the prompt, model, seed, date and persona for every image you keep. If someone disputes a post, the log proves it's synthetic and original.

Two content tiers

Decide now what goes where. Mainstream platforms don't allow nudity, and posting the wrong tier is the fastest way to lose an account.

TierWhat it isWhere it can go
PublicLifestyle, fashion, fitness, travel. Flirty is fine; nudity is not.Instagram, TikTok, Threads, YouTube, Facebook, X
RestrictedAnything more revealing than a mainstream platform allows.Only platforms that explicitly allow it, with age verification and their AI-creator rules followed. Never on mainstream social.
Hard lines

Deepfake and non-consensual imagery laws (e.g. the US TAKE IT DOWN Act, the UK Online Safety Act and the EU AI Act's transparency rules) target exactly the mistakes above. Treat them as non-negotiable. This guide isn't legal advice; check the rules where you live and on every platform you use.

Why the identity lock matters

A text prompt alone invents a slightly different face on every render. Your audience follows a person, so a face that drifts between posts kills trust immediately. The fix has two parts:

The dataset (Step 04)

Shows the model one face in many situations: different angles, lighting, outfits and places.

The LoRA (Step 05)

A small add-on model that learns that face and recalls it whenever you use a made-up trigger word.

Time and money, roughly

~10 hto build and lock the persona the first time
4–6 hper week to produce and post 7 pieces
~$1–5to rent a cloud GPU for one LoRA training run
15×a video costs about 15× an image, so iterate on stills

Estimates for a beginner on the home-PC route. Hosted tools trade time for a monthly fee; check current pricing before you commit.

Step 01Design

Design the persona

The persona bible is the single source of truth. Every image prompt and every caption is built from it. Write it once, then never change the physical description.

Time1–2 hours
You needA niche and a notebook
OutputA one-page persona bible

Start with the niche, not the face

People follow an account for what it gives them: outfit ideas, gym motivation, travel inspiration, a sense of humour. Pick a niche you can make 7 posts a week about for a year, then design a person who'd live in it.

Example persona bible

AL

Ava Lane

26 · fictional · AI creator
NicheEveryday lifestyle, fitness and weekend travel
Trigger wordavlx woman
VoiceWarm, playful, a bit sarcastic
Identity (never changes)

26-year-old woman, oval face, high cheekbones, hazel-green eyes, light freckles across the nose, long wavy chestnut-brown hair with soft curtain bangs, small beauty mark above left lip, fair warm skin, slim athletic build.

Photo style

Raw, unedited phone photo; candid; natural skin texture with visible pores; natural, uneven light; slight sensor noise.

Personality

Morning-coffee person, gym four times a week, weekend trips. Self-deprecating humour.

Wardrobe

oversized knitslinen setsslip dressesathleisuredenim

Recurring locations

sunlit apartmentcafé window seatgymbeach boardwalkrooftop at dusk

Content pillars

get ready with mecoffee & morningsgymtravelgolden hour

Write a distinctive identity

Do

  • Use concrete anchors: eye colour, exact hair colour, length and cut, build
  • Add one or two signature marks, like freckles or a beauty mark
  • Invent a rare trigger token (e.g. avlx) plus "woman" or "man"
  • Fix 3–5 recurring locations so the world feels real

Avoid

  • Vague words like "beautiful" or "stunning", which produce the generic AI face
  • Real names, celebrity references or "looks like…"
  • Changing hair colour or features later, which breaks the identity lock
  • Any youthful descriptor, ever
Step 02Setup

Choose your tools and set up

There are two ways to do this. The home-PC route is free per image and gives you full control. The cloud route needs no graphics card and gets you started in an afternoon. The steps in this guide work for both.

Time1 hour (PC) · 15 min (cloud)
You needA GPU with 8 GB+, or a browser
OutputA working image generator

Route A: home PC

Best value and most control once it's set up.

  • App: ComfyUI (Windows portable, NVIDIA build)
  • Model: an SDXL photoreal checkpoint, e.g. RealVisXL V5.0 or Juggernaut XL
  • Add-ons: ComfyUI-Manager, IPAdapter Plus, Impact Pack (FaceDetailer)
  • Cost per image: electricity only

Route B: cloud, no GPU

Fastest start. You pay per credit or per month.

  • Option 1: rent a GPU by the hour (RunPod, Vast.ai) and run ComfyUI there
  • Option 2: a hosted generator with a "character" or face-reference feature
  • Check: commercial-use rights, content policy, export without watermark

Hardware for the home-PC route

PartMinimumComfortableWhy it matters
GPU (NVIDIA)8 GB VRAM (e.g. RTX 2070/3060)12–24 GBSets which models fit and how fast images render
System RAM16 GB + pagefile on SSD32 GBModel loading spikes RAM; without a pagefile, ComfyUI crashes
Storage100 GB free SSD250 GB+Each checkpoint is 6–7 GB; renders add up quickly

Setting up ComfyUI

  1. Download and extractGet ComfyUI Windows portable (NVIDIA) from the ComfyUI GitHub releases and extract it to a short path on an SSD, e.g. C:\AI\ComfyUI.
  2. Start itRun run_nvidia_gpu.bat. The interface opens in your browser at 127.0.0.1:8188. If you see out-of-memory errors, add --lowvram to the launch line.
  3. Add a checkpointPut your SDXL model in models\checkpoints\. A "Lightning" variant renders in 6 steps (10–20 s per image on 8 GB); the full model is slower but has more natural skin. On 8 GB cards stick to SDXL: Flux runs, but at 1.5–3 minutes per image.
  4. Install the ManagerIf there's no Manager button, install ComfyUI-Manager. You'll use it to add IPAdapter in Step 04.

Step 03Identity

Casting: find the face

Generate a batch of simple headshots from the identity description alone, then choose one hero face. This is the cheapest moment to change your mind, so take your time.

Time~30 minutes
You needPersona bible + generator
OutputOne hero headshot
  1. Render about 40 headshotsUse the identity plus a neutral setup: front-facing, plain background, soft even light. Change only the seed between images.
  2. Shortlist fiveLook for a face that's attractive but specific: something you'd recognise again. Generic "model faces" are hard to lock.
  3. Pick oneIt should be clear, front-facing, evenly lit, with clean eyes and teeth and nothing odd. Save it as your hero image (e.g. hero_ava.png).
  4. Not happy?Edit the identity text, not the seed, and render again. Every face after this point is built from the hero.
Casting prompt close-up portrait photo of a 26 year old woman, oval face, high cheekbones, hazel green eyes, light freckles across the nose, long wavy chestnut brown hair with soft curtain bangs, small beauty mark above left lip, looking at camera, neutral expression, plain light grey wall, soft window light, raw unedited phone photo, natural skin texture with visible pores

Negative: smooth skin, beauty filter, airbrushed, heavy makeup, hdr, oversaturated, cartoon, illustration
Pick with your phone

Look at the shortlist on your phone at Instagram size. That's how your audience will see the face, and odd features are easier to spot there than on a big monitor.

Step 04Identity

Build the reference dataset

Now produce the same face in many different situations. You'll use the hero image as a visual reference through IPAdapter, a node that steers each render toward a given face.

Time2–3 hours
You needHero image + IPAdapter
Output25–40 curated images
  1. Install IPAdapterIn ComfyUI-Manager, install ComfyUI_IPAdapter_plus and restart. Download the SDXL PLUS FACE (portraits) model into models\ipadapter\ and the ViT-H CLIP vision model into models\clip_vision\.
  2. Wire the referenceLoad Checkpoint → IPAdapter Unified Loader → IPAdapter Advanced (with your hero image) → KSampler. Start with weight 0.65 and end_at 0.8 so the last steps can draw natural skin.
  3. Render about 45 varied imagesVary one thing at a time: shot type, angle, lighting, outfit, location, expression.
  4. Curate hardKeep 25–40. Whatever you keep gets baked into the persona permanently.

Coverage to aim for

CategoryInclude
FramingClose-up (~40%), head and shoulders, waist-up, full body (~20%)
AnglesFront, ¾ left, ¾ right, profile, slightly above, slightly below
LightWindow daylight, golden hour, overcast outdoor, warm indoor lamp, flash at night
Context3–5 outfits and 4–6 backgrounds from the bible; a few different expressions

Keep the image if

  • It's unmistakably the same face
  • Eyes, teeth, hands and ears are clean
  • It adds something new: angle, light or setting

Reject the image if

  • It's near-identical to one you've kept
  • The skin looks waxy or airbrushed
  • There's any distortion, even a small one
Fix realism now, not later

The LoRA learns whatever look the dataset has. A plastic-looking dataset gives you a plastic-looking persona forever. If images look "too AI", read Step 07 before you train.

Step 05Identity

Train the persona LoRA

A LoRA is a small add-on file (tens of megabytes) that teaches your checkpoint one new concept: your persona's face, recalled by the trigger word. It's what makes post #300 look like post #1.

Time1 h (cloud) · 3–5 h (8 GB PC)
You needCurated dataset + captions
Outputpersona_v1.safetensors

1. Prepare captions

Give each image a text file with the same name. Start with the trigger, then describe the scene, not the face. That teaches the model that only the person belongs to the trigger word, while clothes and backgrounds stay flexible.

Example caption · img_014.txt avlx woman, sitting at a café window seat holding a coffee cup, cream knit sweater, overcast daylight, waist-up, candid

Use folder naming like img\10_avlx woman\ for kohya-style trainers, where 10 is the number of repeats per epoch.

2. Choose where to train

On your PC

Use kohya_ss (bmaltais GUI), OneTrainer or AI-Toolkit. Possible on 8 GB with the settings below. Close everything else, and expect a few hours.

Rent a GPU recommended

On RunPod or Vast.ai, start an RTX 4090 with a kohya_ss template. Upload the dataset, train, and download the file. Usually about an hour and a few dollars.

3. Settings that work for SDXL on 8 GB

SettingValueNote
Base modelYour SDXL checkpointOr base SDXL 1.0 for wider compatibility
Network dim / alpha16 / 8Enough capacity for one face
Resolution1024, bucketing onMixed aspect ratios are fine
Batch size1Raise it on bigger GPUs
Optimizer · learning rateAdafactor · 1e-4relative_step off
Train text encoderOffUNet only saves memory
Mixed precisionfp16Use bf16 on RTX 30-series and newer
Gradient checkpointing · cache latentsOn · on (to disk)Both are essential on 8 GB
Total steps3,000–4,000≈ images × repeats × epochs; save every 2 epochs
Save checkpoints along the way

Saving every couple of epochs gives you several versions to compare in Step 06. The last one isn't always the best. Over-trained LoRAs copy the dataset's outfits and backgrounds.

Step 06Identity

The consistency test

Before making a single post, prove the identity holds. Render the same 10 very different scenes with fixed seeds, and check whether they show the same person.

Time20 minutes per version
You needLoRA + 10 test prompts
OutputA pass, or a clear fix

Use scenes the dataset didn't contain: a night market, a ski slope, a library, a kitchen, a car interior. Fixed seeds mean you can compare LoRA versions or strengths fairly, because only the LoRA changes.

ResultWhat to do
Same face in 8+ of 10Pass. Lock this LoRA version and strength in the persona bible.
Face drifts between scenesRaise LoRA strength toward 1.0, or retrain with more close-ups.
Every image looks like a dataset photoLower strength to ~0.7, use an earlier epoch, or retrain with more variety.
Looks right but plasticGo to Step 07. A realism pass fixes most of this without retraining.

Step 07Content

Prompting for realism

The goal isn't a perfect photo. It's a believable one. Real influencer content is shot on phones, in real places, with imperfect light. Prompt for that.

The prompt formula

trigger+ shot type+ action / pose+ outfit+ location & light+ photo style
Example · Monday coffee post avlx woman, medium shot, sitting on a windowsill holding a coffee mug, oversized white shirt, messy hair, small apartment with plants, morning sunlight, raw unedited iPhone photo, candid and unposed, natural skin texture with visible pores, slight sensor noise

Negative: smooth skin, beauty filter, glossy skin, porcelain skin, oversaturated, hdr, bokeh, perfect symmetry, airbrushed

Words that add realism

raw unedited phone photocandidvisible poresnatural uneven lightingslight sensor noisereal-world surroundingsfine facial hair

Words that scream "AI"

masterpiece8kultra detailedinstagram aestheticbokehstudio lightingflawless skin

Fixing the "obviously AI" look

SymptomUsual causeFix
Waxy, airbrushed skinFace reference active until the last stepsIPAdapter end_at 0.8, weight 0.6–0.65; add a hires pass without the reference
Mushy face in full-body shotsFace too small at base resolutionHires pass at 1.5× (denoise 0.35–0.4), then FaceDetailer
Glossy "photoshoot" lookPrompt words like studio, bokeh, 8kSwap them for phone-photo wording
Over-saturated, harsh contrastCFG too highLightning models: CFG 1–1.5. Full models: 3.5–5
Face looks pasted onReference weight too highLower IPAdapter weight to 0.5–0.6
Everything too cleanNo imperfectionsAdd subtle grain on export; mess up the scene a little
Biggest single upgrade

FaceDetailer (from ComfyUI-Impact-Pack) finds the face, re-renders it at high resolution and blends it back. It's the best fix for waist-up and full-body shots. Optionally add a realism LoRA ("amateur photo", "skin detail") at 0.3–0.6 strength.

Step 08Content

From photo to video

Short vertical video is where reach lives. Video is expensive, so the rule is simple: iterate on cheap stills, and animate only the frames you've already approved.

Time~1 hour per week
You needApproved stills + a video model
Output5–10 s clips

Always image-to-video

Start every clip from an approved still of your persona. Text-to-video invents a new face; image-to-video keeps yours. Generate 4–8 candidate first frames per idea, pick the best one, then animate it.

Motion prompt · keep it to one action slow push-in, she glances up from her coffee and smiles at the camera, hair moves slightly in the breeze, natural handheld phone movement

Model options

ModelStrengthsUse it for
KlingStrong human motion and facesA good default for persona clips
VeoThe most cinematic and realisticHero clips. Often the strictest content filters
Hailuo / MiniMax, SeedanceCheaper and fasterB-roll, loops, quick tests
Wan (open weights)Runs locally with enough VRAMUnlimited iteration if your GPU can handle it

Run a bake-off once: put the same still through each model with the same motion prompt, then standardise on the winner. Models update often, so repeat it every few months.

Building longer videos

  • Clips come out at about 5–10 seconds. Chain them by using the last frame of clip 1 as the first frame of clip 2.
  • Cut 2–4 clips into a 7–20 second short, vertical 9:16 at 1080×1920.
  • Put a text hook in the first second; add captions and audio inside each app to stay on licensed music.
  • For a voice, pick one text-to-speech voice and never change it. Voice is part of the identity too.
Step 09Content

Quality control and export

One bad hand in one post gets screenshotted and becomes the comment section. A two-minute check per image is the cheapest insurance you have.

QC checklist: reject if any fail

  • Hands and fingers: count them
  • Eyes (matching, same direction), teeth, earrings
  • Face matches the persona; no drift
  • No garbled text, signs or logos
  • Straight lines stay straight (doors, tiles, railings)
  • Video: no flicker, morphing or melting hands
  • Nothing that could read as underage
  • Right tier for the platform it's going to

Fixing small defects

Don't throw away a great image for one bad finger. Use inpainting: mask the problem area and regenerate only that part with the same prompt.

For a sharper final version of a winning draft, re-render it with the same seed plus a hires pass.

Export rules

  • Export as JPEG and strip all metadata. Generator PNGs contain your full prompts and workflow.
  • Add subtle grain to posts only, never to training images.

Platform sizes

IncomeGrowth

Monetisation options

Monetise once you have consistent engagement, not just followers. A few thousand engaged followers in a clear niche are worth more than 50,000 random ones.

1

Brand partnerships

Paid posts featuring products in your niche. Disclose them as ads (#ad or the paid-partnership tag), and tell the brand up front that the creator is AI.

2

Affiliate links

A commission on products the persona "uses": outfits, gym gear, coffee kit. Works from week 3 with a link-in-bio page.

3

Digital products

Photo presets, wallpapers, guides, prompt packs or a newsletter. You set the price and don't depend on a sponsor.

4

Subscriptions

Exclusive content on a platform that explicitly allows AI creators. Follow its labelling and age-verification rules.

5

Platform payouts

Creator funds and ad-revenue programmes. Small at first, but they grow with reach. Check each programme's AI policy.

6

Services

Once it works, sell the skill: build personas or content for brands that want their own AI ambassador.

Read the small print

Payment processors, ad networks and platforms each have their own rules on AI-generated people. Read them before you build income on one, and spread your income across at least two sources.

Appendix AReference

Troubleshooting

The problems almost everyone hits in the first two weeks, and what to do about them.

ProblemFix
CUDA out of memoryAdd --lowvram to the launch file. Use a Lightning checkpoint. Close other GPU apps, including games and browsers with video.
ComfyUI closes while loading a modelThe system ran out of RAM. Enable a pagefile on an SSD, close apps, or upgrade to 32 GB.
Images look fried or over-sharpenedSampler settings don't match the checkpoint. Lightning: ~6 steps, CFG 1–1.5, dpmpp_sde + karras.
"Checkpoint not found"The filename in your workflow must match the file in models\checkpoints exactly.
Every persona looks like the same stock modelMake the identity more distinctive: freckles, a beauty mark, a specific haircut, an unusual eye colour.
Face changes between postsYou're not using the LoRA, or it's too weak. Re-run the consistency test (Step 06).
Outfits or backgrounds repeat in every imageThe LoRA is over-trained. Use an earlier epoch or lower strength, and caption scenes more fully.
Video face melts mid-clipUse a close or medium shot as the first frame, one simple action, and a shorter clip.
Reach dropped suddenlyCheck for a missed AI label, links posted too early, or a borderline post. Remove it and slow down for a week.
Appendix BReference

Glossary

Checkpoint: the main image model file (e.g. an SDXL model). Everything is generated from it.

LoRA: a small add-on file that teaches a checkpoint one new thing, like your persona's face.

Trigger word: a made-up token (e.g. avlx woman) that switches the LoRA's concept on in a prompt.

IPAdapter: a ComfyUI node that uses an image, such as your hero face, as a visual reference.

Seed: the random number behind an image. Same seed plus same settings gives the same image.

CFG: how strictly the model follows the prompt. Too high looks harsh and over-saturated.

Steps · sampler: how many refinement passes the model makes, and the method it uses.

Hires pass: upscale an image and redraw it lightly, which adds detail to skin and eyes.

Denoise: how much a hires or inpainting pass is allowed to change (0 = nothing, 1 = everything).

Inpainting: regenerating only a masked part of an image, like a hand or an eye.

FaceDetailer: automatically re-renders the face at high resolution and blends it back.

Image-to-video: animating an existing still, which keeps the persona's face much better than text-to-video.

VRAM: your graphics card's memory. It limits which models you can run.

Epoch: one full pass over the training dataset when training a LoRA.
